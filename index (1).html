PCB Design · Power Electronics · Embedded

Five-Finger Robotic Hand ControllerIn progress

SIG Robotics, UIUC · Electrical team · 2026

A single controller board for a tendon-driven robotic hand: an ESP32-S3 talking to ten DYNAMIXEL servos over one shared half-duplex bus, with a separate high-current motor rail so stall events never brown out the logic. USB-C for programming, XT30 for battery, and everything fused and sequenced in between.

My role I own the power schematic and battery selection — the fused and reverse-protected input, the 3.3 V logic supply, the 20 A point-of-load module feeding the two motor rails, and the enable/power-good handshake that holds the motors off until logic is up. I also assigned footprints across the board and built the BOM with real manufacturer part numbers for JLCPCB assembly.

  • ESP32-S3-WROOM-1
  • 10× DYNAMIXEL
  • 15 A fused input
  • 20 A point-of-load
  • Dual 10 A motor rails
  • KiCad 10 · JLCPCB

Analog Design · Instrumentation · Signal Processing

Sonar Direction Finder

ECE 145 · Spring 2026 · with Aadi Kenchammana

A direction finder built entirely from analog parts — no microcontroller anywhere in the signal path. A 40 kHz transducer pings the room, three microphones in a 7 cm L-array listen, and the system reads direction from the relative loudness of the returns rather than their timing, because at that array spacing the arrival differences are far too small to resolve.

My role I led two of the four milestones. For signal generation I designed a Schmitt trigger inverter oscillator on ±10 V rails and, when the transducer's current draw dragged the oscillator to 16 MHz, added a push-pull stage so the speaker could be driven without loading the timing node. For the division stage I put an AD531J analog multiplier inside an op-amp feedback loop so it solves for the quotient in real time — turning two microphone amplitudes into the single ratio the comparators threshold on.

What it taught me Nearly every problem was a real-world non-ideality that simulation had hidden. Breadboard parasitic capacitance pulled the oscillator off target, so the calculated 3083 Ω resistor became 2200 Ω. The LM358's gain-bandwidth product capped usable gain near 10× at 40 kHz, so the intended 100× had to be split across two stages. The microphones behaved as current sources and produced nothing usable until a 10 kΩ resistor to ground converted the output to a voltage. And the speaker was coupling straight into the receive chain until a 150 Hz low-pass filter cleaned it up.

  • 42.8 kHz
  • 19.5 Vpp
  • ±10 V rails
  • 3× MA40S4R, 7 cm L-array
  • ≈55× two-stage gain
  • 4-LED direction display

Resistor-Key Ignition Security System

ECE 110 Final Project · Spring 2026 · Group 8

An anti-hot-wiring ignition built around a physical resistor as the key. A Wheatstone bridge turns the key's resistance into a voltage that must land inside a 0.4 V window, and an RC delay forces the correct value to be held continuously for two seconds before the motor is allowed to turn — so an attacker sweeping a potentiometer gets only four or five attempts in ten seconds.

My role I designed both comparator modules. I used the LM311P in open-loop configuration for its open-collector output, which needs a pull-up resistor to have any path to a HIGH level — one pair brackets the key voltage between the upper and lower bounds, and a second pair watches the delay capacitors and fires once they cross the 4.5 V reference.

  • 9 V supply
  • 0.4 V key window (2.09–2.49 V)
  • τ = 3.0 s → 2.08 s trip
  • LM311P comparators
  • MOSFET AND gate + flyback
  • Starts in < 3 s
